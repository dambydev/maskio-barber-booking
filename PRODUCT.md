# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary users are local clients of Maskio Barber Concept in and around San Giovanni Rotondo, mostly mobile-first visitors who want to understand the quality of the barber shop and book quickly. They are often goal-oriented and time-conscious: they need trust, clarity, and a direct path to reservation without decoding a complex site.

Secondary users include returning clients who already know the shop and use the site mainly as a fast booking/contact gateway.

## Product Purpose

Maskio Barber Concept presents the shop, builds confidence in its hair-focused craft, and converts visitors toward online booking. Success means a visitor immediately understands that the experience is premium, modern, and reliable, with hair services at the center and beard services mentioned as a supporting offer rather than exaggerated into a separate brand promise.

## Brand Personality

Premium, confident, and contemporary. The tone should feel direct and professional, with a bold luxury barber attitude grounded in real service quality rather than inflated claims. The emotional goal is to make clients feel they are choosing a polished, expert, masculine grooming experience with strong attention to style.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS-style landing pages, sterile dashboards, or template-like card grids.
- Overdone barber clichés: excessive moustache/beard iconography, too many scissors/razors as decoration, fake vintage tropes, or copy that makes beard care feel more central than hair.
- AI-slop visuals: repeated identical cards, inconsistent banners, noisy gradients everywhere, decorative effects that compete with booking clarity.
- Any redesign that hides the existing hero video, central logo, or direct booking path.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with hair-focused craft: communicate cut, styling, and personal look first; mention beard services only proportionally.
2. Premium before decorative: black/gold identity, real imagery, and confident spacing should carry the brand more than gimmicks.
3. Make booking obvious: every major section should support trust or conversion without changing the booking flow.
4. Keep the cinematic hero recognizable: the homepage opens with video, centered logo, and central statement.
5. Unify system elements: banners, CTAs, cards, and section rhythm should feel like one brand instead of separate styles.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Aim for WCAG AA basics: strong text contrast on dark backgrounds, clear focus states, readable mobile typography, no horizontal overflow, and reduced visual noise. Motion should remain purposeful and should not block access to content; visual changes should preserve readability for mobile and low-light usage.
